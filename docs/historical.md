# Former countries and teams of sport

Made by `pnpm data`; a test fails if this list and the data differ. A medal table, a record book or a history of a sport
still names countries that no longer exist, and Olympic teams that are not countries. Hata has the flag of 12 former
countries, each under a code that no present-day country uses, and lists 10 more codes with the reason there is no flag.
The decisions behind them are in [decisions.md](decisions.md), "Former countries".

**The code of a former country** is its ISO 3166-3 code where that standard has one (four letters: `SUHH`, `DDDE`, `YUCS`), else the
IOC's (`FRG`, `BOH`, `WIF`). **The other codes that find it** are `FLAG_ALIASES`: the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 and alpha-3 the place
had, and the IOC's three letters. `flagCode`, `flag`, `flagName`, `manifest`, `leftOut` and `<hata-flag>` read them in any case;
`isFlagCode` and `FLAG_CODES` hold only the code itself. No alias is a code of a place, and no alias belongs to two.

## Codes with no flag

| Code | Team or place | Years | Also found by | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `AIN` | Individual Neutral Athletes (個人の中立選手) | 2024 to 2024 | none | The neutral athletes at Paris 2024 competed under the Olympic flag, the IOC's mark, so there is no flag to ship. A site draws a badge with the code. |
| `ANZ` | Australasia (オーストララシア) | 1908 to 1912 | none | The combined team of Australia and New Zealand at the Games of 1908 and 1912 had no flag of its own, and the Australian and New Zealand flags of the time are not one picture, so there is none to ship. A site draws a badge with the code. |
| `CS` | Czechoslovakia or Serbia and Montenegro (チェコスロバキアまたはセルビア・モンテネグロ) | 1974 to 2006 | none | CS is ambiguous: ISO 3166-1 gave it to Czechoslovakia until 1992 and to Serbia and Montenegro from 2003, so it is not an alias of either, because a near miss would draw the wrong country. Ask for Czechoslovakia by `CSHH`, `CSK` or `TCH`, and for Serbia and Montenegro by `CSXX` or `SCG`. |
| `EOR` | Refugee Olympic Team (難民選手団) | 2016 to 2024 | `ROT` | The Refugee Olympic Team (2016, where the IOC's code was ROT, and from 2020, EOR) competes under the Olympic flag, the IOC's mark, not a country's, so there is no flag to ship. A site draws a badge with the code. |
| `EUA` | United Team of Germany (ドイツ合同チーム) | 1956 to 1964 | none | The United Team of Germany (the Games of 1956, 1960 and 1964) flew the German tricolour with the white Olympic rings across its middle, a flag that carries the IOC's mark, so it is not shipped. East and West Germany, which entered separate teams from 1968, are DDDE and FRG; a site that needs a mark for this row draws a badge with the code. |
| `EUN` | Unified Team (合同チーム) | 1992 to 1992 | none | The Unified Team of the former Soviet republics (the Winter and Summer Games of 1992) competed under the Olympic flag, the IOC's own mark, not a country's: it has no flag of its own, and the package does not ship the Olympic rings. A site that needs a mark for this row draws a badge with the code. |
| `IOP` | Independent Olympic Participants (独立オリンピック参加者) | 1992 to 1992 | none | The athletes from Yugoslavia (and Macedonia) who competed independently in 1992 marched under the Olympic flag, the IOC's mark, so there is no flag to ship. A site draws a badge with the code. |
| `OAR` | Olympic Athletes from Russia (ロシアからのオリンピック選手) | 2018 to 2018 | none | The athletes at Pyeongchang 2018 competed under the Olympic flag, the IOC's mark, so there is no flag to ship. A site draws a badge with the code. |
| `ROC` | Russian Olympic Committee (ロシア・オリンピック委員会) | 2020 to 2022 | none | The Russian Olympic Committee's team at Tokyo 2020 and Beijing 2022 marched under the committee's own emblem on white, a federation's mark and not a country's flag (Russia's flag was not allowed), so it is not shipped. A site draws a badge with the code. |
| `RU1` | Russian Empire (ロシア帝国) | 1900 to 1912 | none | The Russian Empire's teams of 1900, 1908 and 1912 are listed under one IOC code, RU1, but the Empire had no single flag in sporting use: the black, yellow and white state flag, the white, blue and red tricolour of its merchant ships and the imperial standard were each flown, and sources differ on which a team carried. Left out rather than chosen for it; a site draws a badge with the code. |
