The armies of the Sengoku period went to battle under banners: the tall narrow *nobori*, the flags called *hata*, and the *uma-jirushi*, the sign carried beside a commander. Hata has forty-four of them, as their own import, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/samurai`, so a page that draws countries only does not download them. An id is `samurai:` and a lower-case slug, such as `samurai:sanada-rokumonsen`; no country's or region's code contains a colon, so a banner can never take the place of one, and `isFlagCode("samurai:ii-igeta")` is false.

```mjs
import { frame } from "@johnmorrisdotca/hata";
import { samuraiFlag, samuraiInfo, samuraiName } from "@johnmorrisdotca/hata/samurai";

// A banner is an SVG string, framed like a country's; its id begins with "samurai:", and its record says what is documented.
const nobori = await samuraiFlag("samurai:sanada-rokumonsen");
const round = nobori === null ? "" : frame(nobori, { shape: "round", label: samuraiName("samurai:sanada-rokumonsen", "ja") ?? "" });
console.log(nobori?.startsWith("<svg"), round.includes("clipPath"), samuraiName("samurai:sanada-rokumonsen", "ja"));
console.log(samuraiInfo("samurai:sanada-rokumonsen")?.status);
```

`samuraiFlag(id)` loads the SVG string (a promise), `samuraiName(id, lang)` and `samuraiAbout(id, lang)` the name and a sentence in English or Japanese, `samuraiAspect(id)` the width over the height, `samuraiInfo(id)` everything the package knows of it, and `samuraiIn("nobori")` the ids of one kind (`nobori`, `hata` or `uma`). `samuraiId("ii-igeta")` reads an id written loosely, and `SAMURAI_IDS` lists them all. Each returns `null` for an id with no banner here.

## Size, and loading one drawing

The entry carries the records only: the ids, names, descriptions, dates, sources and notes, about 85 KB (11 KB gzipped), with no drawing in it. Each drawing is a module of its own, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/samurai/ii-igeta` (a median of 388 B, at most 4.8 KB, the lettered ones the largest), which the lookup loads by a dynamic import when it is asked for, as `/load` does for a country's flag. A page therefore carries the drawings it asks for and no others. You can import a module yourself (`import svg from "@johnmorrisdotca/hata/samurai/ii-igeta"`, the drawing of `samurai:ii-igeta`); there is no other way to a drawing than its id or its module.

## What is documented, and what is not

Almost no banner of the period survives, and none is drawn here from a surviving cloth. Nine designs are the ones a reference gives in words: the Japanese Wikipedia's article on uma-jirushi (馬印), which lists the banners and signs of named commanders (Uesugi Kenshin's red disc on dark blue, Hojo Ujiyasu's five-colour steps, Date Masamune's large black banner, Toyotomi Hideyoshi's gold war fan with a red streamer, Tokugawa Ieyasu's gold fan, Oda Nobunaga's gold parasol, Ii Naomasa's gold well-curb on red), and its article on the Sanada clan (真田氏), which describes the Sanada nobori, all red with the six-coin crest. The words give the design and the ground, not the proportions, the shades or the order of the parts, so each drawing is a `reconstruction`, and its `note` says which part follows the source and which is this package's. Both pages are encyclopedia articles, and a better source, a museum's or a period record, is wanted for each.

## Drawn from a picture of about the period: the handscroll 御馬印

Thirty-five more are drawn from a handscroll of about the period: 御馬印 (*Go-umajirushi*), in six scrolls, in the National Diet Library (scroll 1 [NDL 1286895](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1286895), scroll 2 [NDL 1288242](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1288242), public domain). The library dates it to around the Kan'ei years (1624 to 1644); that is its estimate, and the scroll calls Tokugawa Hidetada by his posthumous name 台徳院, given in 1632, so it was made after that. It paints the banners and standards of the shogun and the daimyo, each lord's name beside his. The cloth's proportions and the place and size of its parts are measured from the photograph of the frame each record names (`source.url` opens that frame), so these are `documented`: Taitoku-in's banner with 五, the Lord of Mito's gold disc on black, the Lord of Echizen's red disc on white, the pale blue nobori with twenty-seven black dots of Hoshina, titled Higo-no-kami, three of Sakai Kunai's, Uesugi Danjō's gold disc on black and his banner with 毘, the three white discs on black of Tōdō of Tsu, titled Izumi-no-kami, the nobori with 本 and the gold band on black of Honda, titled Mino-no-kami; and, in the second batch, the plain red nobori and the plain red banner of Ii, titled Kamon-no-kami; Koide, titled Yamato-no-kami's nobori parted white and black on a slant and his red disc on white; the three black discs on white of Terazawa, titled Shima-no-kami; the six coins on black (labelled 大馬しるし) and the red band on white of Sanada, titled Kawachi-no-kami; the white disc on black of Kōriki, titled Settsu-no-kami; the gold lozenge on black of Matsudaira, titled Shimōsa-no-kami, on a nobori and a banner; the three stacked lozenges on black (labelled 大馬しるし) of Ogasawara, titled Ukon-no-taifu; and the six black bands on white of Matsudaira, titled Tanba-no-kami; and, in the third batch, the white disc on black of Sakai, titled Uta-no-kami, and the two gold zigzags on black of Sakai, titled Awa-no-kami, from scroll 1; and from scroll 2 (NDL 1288242), the red ring and disc on white and the plain gold banner of Matsudaira, titled Kai-no-kami, the gold band on red on a slant of Matsudaira, titled Echigo-no-kami, the nine white discs on red and the gold ring on black of Matsudaira, titled Yamashiro-no-kami, the white disc on black of Matsudaira, titled Dewa-no-kami, on a banner and a nobori, the white ring on black of Ōkubo, titled Kaga-no-kami, and the pale blue nobori with 山 of Aoyama, titled Ōkura-no-shō. The shades are this package's reading of the painting, and the loops that hold the cloth to the pole, the pole and any finial are not drawn. The drawings keep the painting's orientation, with the pole side on the right as painted, so a slanted band or a lean is described as it is seen in the drawing. The characters 五, 毘, 本 and 山 are lettered in Yuji Syuku, an SIL Open Font License brush font named in `NOTICE.md`, not traced from the page, so their brush is the font's; the painter writes Uesugi Danjō's character as the variant 毗, which the font does not have.

Still left out: Uesugi Kenshin's own 毘, Takeda Shingen's Fūrinkazan and Tokugawa Ieyasu's 厭離穢土欣求浄土, because the scroll read here does not show them; and, still to come, the banners with a detailed crest such as birds or leaves, the hanging ornaments, and the three-dimensional standards. A banner is a historical picture, not a mark to use for a person or a family today. Continue with [codes and names](codes-and-names.md).
