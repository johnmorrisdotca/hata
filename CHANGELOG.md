### Added

- **The US Navy's special signal pennants** (MODULES-1819), in the `/signals` entry as a group of their own, `tactical`, under ids `usn:<slug>`: the code or answering pennant, Corpen (course), Designation, Emergency, Formation, Interrogative, Negative, Preparative, Screen, Speed, Starboard, Station, Subdivision, Turn and four substitutes. 18 flags, so `SIGNAL_IDS` has 58 (the Code's 40 first). ## [1.16.0] - 2026-11-24

### Added

- **Every former country's flag: all 30 ISO 3166-3 codes, and the larger states that never had one** (MODULES-1808). 33 more flags (45 former countries in all, 512 flags): 15 ISO 3166-3 codes (`AIDJ`, `BYAA`, `DYBJ`, `FQHH`, `GEHH`, `HVBF`, `JTUM`, `MIUM`, `NQAQ`, `PCHH`, `PUUM`, `PZPA`, `VDVN`, `WKUM`, `YDYE`) and 18 states under codes of Hata's own, `X` and two letters (Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, Prussia, the Kingdom of Hawaii, the German Empire, South Vietnam, the Korean Empire, the Qing Empire, the United Arab Republic, the Republic of Texas, the Kingdom of Italy, the Sultanate of Zanzibar, the Papal States, the Kingdom of Sardinia, Siam, Tibet (1912 to 1950), Manchukuo and the Confederate States of America, whose three national flags are dated variants; the battle flag is not a national flag and is in the military set). Each has dated flags with the legal flag and the flag in use, a source and a licence. Eight more codes are in `LEFT_OUT` with their reasons (six ISO 3166-3 codes, and Gran Colombia and Tanganyika for their licence). `manifest(code).wikidata` is the empty string for a state with no Wikidata item. New guide: [former countries](docs/guides/former-countries.md), in English and Japanese.
- **Ship signal flags: the International Code of Signals** (MODULES-1809). A new entry point, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/signals` (the records, 58 KB; each flag a module of its own, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/signals/<id>`, loaded by the async `signalFlag`), with an id namespace of its own, `ics:`: the 26 alphabet flags, the 10 numeral pennants, the 3 substitutes and the answering pennant, drawn for this package from the Code's own plate (Pub. 102, a United States Government work) as exact geometry under the MIT licence, with each flag's meaning in English (the Code's words) and Japanese. `signalFlag`, `signalName`, `signalMeaning`, `signalAspect`, `signalInfo`, `signalId`, `isSignalId`, `signalsFor` and `SIGNAL_IDS`. `pnpm draw` makes the data from `scripts/draw-signals.ts`. A page of them in the demo (`signals.html`, kinds as chips) and a guide in English and Japanese. The shared parts for the sets drawn for this package are `src/drawn.ts`, `scripts/draw-lib.ts`, `demo/set.*` and `src/sets.test.ts`.
- **Samurai battle banners** (MODULES-1810). A new entry point, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/samurai` (the records, 19 KB; each banner a module of its own, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/samurai/<id>`, loaded by the async `samuraiFlag`), with an id namespace of its own, `samurai:`: nine banners of Sengoku daimyo (nobori, hata and uma-jirushi: Uesugi, Hojo, Date, Toyotomi, Tokugawa, Oda, Sanada and Ii), drawn for this package as geometry under the MIT licence from the words of the Japanese Wikipedia's articles on uma-jirushi and the Sanada clan. No cloth survives, so each is a `reconstruction`, and its `note` says which part follows the source and which is this package's. `samuraiFlag`, `samuraiName`, `samuraiAbout`, `samuraiAspect`, `samuraiInfo`, `samuraiId`, `isSamuraiId`, `samuraiIn` and `SAMURAI_IDS`; `pnpm draw:samurai` makes the data. A page of them in the demo (`samurai.html`, kinds as chips) and a guide in English and Japanese. Left out, with the reason in the guide: the banners that are written characters (Fūrinkazan, 毘, 厭離穢土欣求浄土).
- **Medieval banners** (MODULES-1811). A new entry point, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/medieval`, and one module for each drawing (`@johnmorrisdotca/hata/medieval/<id>`; the entry is 83 KB, 12 KB gzipped, the records only), with an id namespace of its own, `med:`: 42 banners. Six are drawn for this package as geometry under the MIT licence from dated pages (the Templars', the red cross on white, Denmark's in the Gelre Armorial, the arms of Aragon, Scotland's saltire and the Hospitallers' cross); 36 are Wikimedia Commons drawings taken as the country flags are (public domain and CC0 only, through the licence check, optimised, with each file's page, licence and author kept and listed in `NOTICE.md`; `pnpm data:medieval` fetches them): England's Royal Banner, France's blue banner and fleurs-de-lis, France and Navarre, the Holy Roman Empire's red cross and Reichssturmfahne, Genoa, the Teutonic Order, León, Navarre, Portugal's first banner, Hungary's royal standards, Poland's crowned eagle, the Cross of Burgundy, the Byzantine banners, the Papal States, Jerusalem, Florence, Pisa, Milan, Corsica, Serbia, the Almoravids, the Ayyubids, Saladin, Morocco, Owain Glyn Dŵr's dragon, Rostock, the early Swiss cross, Unterwalden and Ragusa. Each is a `reconstruction` with its dates where its file gives them. Left out: the Lion of St Mark (301 KB optimised, over the 140 KB ceiling), and every Commons drawing under CC BY-SA. `medievalFlag`, `medievalName`, `medievalAbout`, `medievalAspect`, `medievalInfo`, `medievalId`, `isMedievalId`, `medievalIn` and `MEDIEVAL_IDS`; `pnpm draw:medieval` makes the data. A page of them in the demo (`medieval.html`, kinds as chips) and a guide in English and Japanese.
- **Military flags: naval ensigns, jacks and war flags of more than thirty navies** (MODULES-1812). A new entry point, `@johnmorrisdotca/hata/military`, and one module for each drawing (`@johnmorrisdotca/hata/military/<id>`), so a page carries only the drawings it asks for (the entry is 99 KB, 11 KB gzipped, the records only), with an id namespace of its own, `mil:`: 60 flags. Five are drawn for this package as geometry under the MIT licence (Japan's Maritime Self-Defense Force ensign and the Imperial Japanese Navy's, the United Kingdom's White Ensign and Union Jack as naval jack, the United States' Navy jack); the others are 55 Wikimedia Commons files taken as the country flags are (public domain and CC0 only, through the licence check, optimised, with each file's page, licence and author kept and listed in `NOTICE.md`; `pnpm data:military` fetches them): the ensigns, jacks and war flags of Germany (1871 to 1892, the Weimar Republic, the Reichsmarine and today's), France, Italy, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, Poland, Greece, Russia, the Soviet Union, India, Pakistan, China, Taiwan, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Thailand, Singapore, South Africa, Egypt, Israel, Ukraine, Colombia, Indonesia (the jack), Argentina, Chile, Mexico, Norway, Portugal, Spain, South Korea, the Philippines, Ireland, Peru and Romania, and the United States' national flags of 1861 to 1865. Each has its dates where its file gives them, its source and a status. Japan's Rising Sun Flag is in this set only, as an ensign among the other navies', and in no country's record. The two Confederate battle flags (the Army of Northern Virginia's and the Navy's jack) are shipped for history and education and withheld by default, as Germany's swastika flag is: `MILITARY_RESTRICTED_IDS`, `militaryFlag(id, { allowRestricted: true })`, `militaryInfo(id)?.restricted`. `militaryFlag`, `militaryName`, `militaryAbout`, `militaryAspect`, `militaryInfo`, `militaryId`, `isMilitaryId`, `militaryIn` and `MILITARY_IDS`; `pnpm draw:military` makes the data. A page of them in the demo (`military.html`, kinds as chips) and a guide in English and Japanese.
- **Six more organisations' flags, taken from Commons, and the rule for the others** (MODULES-1813, John 2026-11-24). A body whose own terms or law explicitly restrict its flag or emblem stays out; a body with no terms found for its flag or emblem may come in, as a public-domain or CC0 Commons drawing through the licence check, with a note saying no terms were found and the day read. In: CARICOM (`org:caricom`), the League of Arab States (`org:arab-league`), the Commonwealth of Independent States (`org:cis`), the Commonwealth of Nations (`org:commonwealth`), the Andean Community (`org:andean-community`) and the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (`org:oic`, its flag of 1981 to 2011). New `organisationSource(id)` gives each file, page, licence and author; `pnpm data:organisations` fetches the files and `pnpm draw:organisations` makes the data. Out because their own terms or law restrict the flag or emblem: the United Nations, NATO, the Red Cross and Red Crescent, the African Union, ASEAN, the Nordic Council, the Olympic rings; out because their pages could not be read (403, not worked around): the Organization of American States and Mercosur; out for want of a drawing: the Pacific Islands Forum. `NOTICE.md` and `docs/decisions.md` record each decision. The guide is updated in English and Japanese.

### Changed

- **The drawn sets and the organisations load one drawing at a time.** `signalFlag`, `samuraiFlag`, `militaryFlag` and `medievalFlag` (new in this release) return a promise, and a new `loadOrganisationFlag` loads any organisation's drawing on demand (`organisationFlag` is unchanged: synchronous, the European Union's drawing, as in 1.15.0): each drawing is a module of its own (`@johnmorrisdotca/hata/<set>/<id>`), loaded by a dynamic import when it is asked for, as `/load` does for a country, so a page carries only the drawings it asks for. Each set's entry holds the records only (signals 58 KB, samurai 19 KB, military 99 KB, medieval 83 KB, organisations 7 KB). The restricted Confederate flags stay withheld unless asked for by name.
- **The main entry is 13 KB** (4.5 KB gzipped), up from 12 KB, for the new codes and aliases; the size test holds the new budget.
- **The terms of the organisations left out were read again** (MODULES-1813). On 2026-11-24 the page of each body listed in `NOTICE.md` as left out was read again, and what it said that day is recorded beside the earlier reading. None of the terms lets an MIT package pass on a drawing made from the description of the flag or emblem, so no organisation is added and the European Union is still the only one in. Three pages could not be read (the ICRC's database page returned no text, ASEAN's answered with a browser check, the Nordic Council's with 403) and the earlier readings stand; none was worked around.
